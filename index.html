One pinhole is a repair. The second one in a year, in a different spot, is a message. Work through this list before deciding whether to patch again or replace the run.

Has more than one pinhole turned up?

Leaks in different spots along the same run, months apart, usually mean the pipe is thin along its whole length, not failing at one point.

Does the water run rusty after sitting overnight?

Rust-colored water first thing in the morning is a common sign of old galvanized steel corroding from the inside.

Is pressure weaker at every tap?

If the kitchen, the bathroom and the basement sink all run weaker than they used to, scale may be narrowing the bore. Galvanized pipe can look fine on the outside while this happens, which is why pressure fades before a leak ever shows.

How old is the house?

The median Harrisburg home was built in 1947, 43 percent of city homes predate 1940 and 66 percent predate 1960. Houses from the first half of the last century are where galvanized supply lines are most often found.

Is the rust coming from the street end?

If the trouble seems to start where the line enters the house, it may be the buried service line rather than the house piping. That is a separate pipe with a separate fix; see water line repair in Harrisburg.

Is it one spot in otherwise sound pipe?

A patch is the right call for a bad fitting, a section hit by a nail, or a length that split in a freeze. If the pipe on both sides is the same age and condition, the next leak tends to show up a few feet along.

Do you know what a repipe involves?

A repipe replaces the supply runs, usually in copper or PEX, often by opening a few spots in plaster or drywall and running new lines through the basement joists. The number of fixtures and floors, exposed versus buried runs, the material, wall repair and the permit all move the job.

Have you checked the permit rule?

Inside the City of Harrisburg, installing or replacing pipe needs a permit and a master plumber licensed with the city, through the Bureau of Codes. The city adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code effective January 1, 2026.
